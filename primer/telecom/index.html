Industry Overview
Telecommunications is the infrastructure layer of the modern economy — connecting consumers, enterprises, and devices via wireless, wireline, and satellite networks. The US market generates ~$500B in annual revenue dominated by three national wireless carriers and a handful of cable broadband incumbents.
📶

Wireless / Mobile

Nationwide cellular networks with 3 major US carriers: T-Mobile, AT&T, Verizon. Postpaid vs prepaid subscriber economics. 5G is the next battleground for network leadership. T-Mobile leads in 5G coverage after the Sprint merger in 2020.

🌐

Broadband & Fixed Line

Cable, DSL, and fiber internet. AT&T Fiber and Verizon Fios compete on fiber. T-Mobile Home Internet is disrupting cable with fixed wireless access (FWA). Charter and Comcast remain cable broadband incumbents with tens of millions of subscribers.

🏢

Enterprise & B2B

SD-WAN, private 5G, UCaaS, and IoT connectivity. Carriers compete with tech vendors for enterprise IT wallet share. AT&T Business, T-Mobile Business, and Verizon Business each target large enterprise and government contracts.

🗼

Tower & Infrastructure

Cell tower REITs: American Tower, Crown Castle, SBA Communications. Carriers lease tower space rather than own. Small cells and DAS (Distributed Antenna Systems) are critical for dense urban 5G coverage at mmWave frequencies.

🛰️

Satellite & Alternative Access

SpaceX Starlink, Amazon Kuiper, OneWeb. T-Mobile x SpaceX direct-to-device partnership targets dead zones. Satellite fills rural coverage gaps where terrestrial build is uneconomic. LEO vs GEO tradeoffs: LEO delivers much lower latency (20-40ms vs 600ms).

💳

MVNOs & Prepaid

Mobile Virtual Network Operators ride on host carrier networks without owning infrastructure. Mint Mobile (T-Mobile), Boost (T-Mobile), TracFone (Verizon). Prepaid competes on price with postpaid; lower ARPU but lower churn cost and no credit exposure.

T-Mobile

Merged with Sprint 2020; captured massive mid-band spectrum. Most 5G coverage in the US (~115M subscribers). "Un-carrier" challenger brand disrupting legacy pricing models. Fastest-growing major carrier in postpaid and FWA.

AT&T

~70M wireless subscribers plus AT&T Fiber for broadband. Divested WarnerMedia (now Warner Bros. Discovery) to refocus on connectivity. FirstNet government contract for first responder network provides stable recurring revenue.

Verizon

mmWave 5G leader in dense urban markets; C-band spectrum for mid-band coverage. 115M total connections. FiOS fiber in select northeast markets. Known for network reliability as primary brand differentiator.

5G Spectrum Bands

Low-band (sub-1GHz): wide coverage, moderate speed. Mid-band (2.5-3.7GHz): capacity + speed sweet spot — T-Mobile advantage. mmWave (24GHz+): extreme speed and capacity, very short range — Verizon dense urban play.

Industry Scale

US telecom generates ~$500B in annual revenue across wireless, wireline, cable, and broadband. Wireless service revenue alone represents ~$200B. Capital expenditure runs $80-100B/year across major carriers for network infrastructure.

FCC Spectrum Auctions

C-band auction closed at $81B in 2021 — the largest spectrum auction in US history. Spectrum is the most critical long-term structural asset. Carriers with more mid-band spectrum have fundamental network capacity advantages.

Key Terminology
Essential vocabulary for telecom — subscriber metrics, network technology, financial KPIs, and competitive dynamics.
ARPU (Average Revenue Per User)Financial▼
The primary revenue metric for wireless carriers — total service revenue divided by average subscriber count. US postpaid ARPU is approximately $50-55 per month. Prepaid ARPU runs $25-35. ARPU growth is constrained by unlimited plan commoditization and promotional competition.
Churn RateSubscriber▼
The percentage of subscribers who cancel or disconnect service monthly. Low churn is the hallmark of a healthy wireless business. Best-in-class postpaid churn is below 0.8% per month. T-Mobile, AT&T, and Verizon have all achieved historically low churn due to installment plan lock-in and promotional switching friction.
Postpaid vs. PrepaidSubscriber▼
Postpaid subscribers pay a monthly bill with a credit check — higher ARPU (~$50-55), lower churn, EIP device financing. Prepaid subscribers pay upfront with no credit check — lower ARPU (~$25-35), higher churn, no device installments. Postpaid is the premium segment; prepaid serves cost-sensitive and credit-constrained customers.
5G NR (New Radio)Technology▼
The ITU standard for fifth-generation cellular networks. Three spectrum tiers: sub-1GHz (low-band) for nationwide coverage and building penetration; mid-band 2.5-3.7GHz for the capacity and speed sweet spot T-Mobile dominates via Sprint spectrum; mmWave 24GHz+ for extreme speed and capacity in dense urban venues — Verizon's initial 5G strategy. Most consumer 5G experience is mid-band.
SpectrumNetwork▼
Radio frequencies licensed by the FCC via auction — the most critical long-term structural asset in wireless. Spectrum is finite; the carrier with more usable mid-band spectrum has a fundamental network capacity advantage. The C-band auction closed at $81B in 2021. T-Mobile acquired vast 2.5GHz mid-band spectrum via the Sprint merger without paying additional auction fees.
MVNO (Mobile Virtual Network Operator)Business Model▼
A carrier that resells host carrier network capacity under its own brand, paying wholesale rates without owning tower infrastructure. T-Mobile hosts Mint Mobile, Boost Mobile, and many others. Verizon hosts TracFone. MVNOs compete on price and brand but have lower margins and no network differentiation. Host carriers earn recurring wholesale revenue from network utilization.
EBITDA / Adjusted EBITDAFinancial▼
Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization — the key profitability metric for telecom. Because carriers are capex-intensive (billions in network equipment depreciated over time), EBITDA better reflects operational cash generation than net income. Adjusted EBITDA strips out stock-based compensation and merger costs. US carriers target EBITDA margins of 35-45%.
Capex IntensityFinancial▼
Capital expenditure divided by total revenue — a measure of how much a carrier must reinvest to maintain and grow its network. US carriers typically run 15-20% capex intensity. 5G buildout years push this higher as carriers deploy new RAN equipment, mid-band spectrum, and densification small cells. Returns on 5G capex remain a key investor question.
Device Subsidy / EIP (Equipment Installment Plan)Commercial▼
Carriers finance smartphone purchases over 24-36 month installment plans. Replaced the old two-year contract model but creates similar lock-in: switching carriers means paying off the outstanding device balance. Promotional credits (trade-in deals, "free" phone offers) are now the primary competitive weapon — effectively subsidizing phones over the installment period to acquire and retain subscribers.
Core NetworkNetwork▼
The central nervous system of a carrier network — EPC (Evolved Packet Core) for 4G LTE and 5GC (5G Core) for 5G. Handles call routing, subscriber authentication, policy enforcement, and data session management. Modern 5G cores are cloud-native and software-defined, deployed on AWS, Azure, or private cloud infrastructure. Enables network slicing and service differentiation.
RAN (Radio Access Network)Network▼
The physical layer of cell towers and base stations that connects devices to the carrier core. RAN is the largest single capex item for carriers. Traditional RAN vendors: Ericsson, Nokia, Samsung. Open RAN (O-RAN) decouples hardware and software, enabling multi-vendor deployments and software-defined automation. Dish/EchoStar built the first major commercial Open RAN network in the US.
Network SlicingTechnology▼
A 5G capability that partitions the physical network into multiple virtual networks (slices) each with dedicated quality-of-service guarantees. A carrier can offer one slice for autonomous vehicles (ultra-low latency), another for video streaming (high bandwidth), and another for IoT sensors (low power, low data). Key monetization pathway for enterprise 5G — still in early commercial deployment.
eSIMTechnology▼
An embedded SIM allowing software-based carrier activation and switching without a physical SIM card. Standard on all modern iPhones and Android flagships. Enables faster MVNO competition and device-centric switching — a subscriber can change carriers without visiting a store. Apple pushing carriers toward eSIM-only devices accelerates this transition and reduces carrier control of the switching process.
Fixed Wireless Access (FWA)Product▼
Home internet service delivered via cellular network rather than a cable or fiber connection. T-Mobile Home Internet ($50/month) is the fastest-growing FWA product, having reached ~6M subscribers. FWA leverages excess mid-band 5G capacity and is highly profitable (near 100% gross margin on incremental network usage). Poses genuine competitive threat to cable broadband in suburban and rural markets.
IMS (IP Multimedia Subsystem)Network▼
The VoLTE (Voice over LTE) and VoNR (Voice over New Radio) infrastructure enabling voice calls over 4G and 5G data networks. IMS replaced circuit-switched voice on modern networks. All US carriers have completed 3G sunset and rely on IMS for voice. Enables HD Voice, Wi-Fi calling, and advanced calling features — critical for carrier-to-carrier interoperability.
RoamingCommercial▼
Using another carrier network when outside home coverage area — domestic (using US Cellular or regional carrier network) or international. Carriers have bilateral roaming agreements with revenue sharing. International roaming was historically a high-margin revenue line; unlimited international plans have eroded per-trip revenue but reduce churn for frequent travelers.
NPS (Net Promoter Score)Customer▼
A customer satisfaction metric measuring likelihood to recommend — a proxy for brand loyalty and churn risk. T-Mobile has historically led major US carriers in NPS, reflecting the "Un-carrier" brand investment. Higher NPS correlates with lower churn and lower customer acquisition costs via referral. JD Power wireless satisfaction studies provide third-party NPS benchmarking.
SD-WANEnterprise▼
Software-Defined Wide Area Network — enterprise connectivity management that abstracts the underlying network (MPLS, broadband, LTE) into a centrally managed overlay. Carriers bundle SD-WAN with connectivity services as a managed enterprise offering. Cisco Meraki, VMware (Broadcom) VeloCloud, and Fortinet compete in this space. Key upsell for carriers moving up the enterprise IT value stack.
UCaaS (Unified Communications as a Service)Enterprise▼
Cloud-delivered voice, video, and messaging replacing on-premise PBX systems. Microsoft Teams, RingCentral, and Cisco Webex are leading UCaaS platforms. Carriers bundle UCaaS with mobile and broadband connectivity for enterprise accounts. UCaaS represents a higher-margin, stickier revenue stream than raw connectivity — a key battleground for carrier enterprise growth.
MNO (Mobile Network Operator)Business Model▼
A carrier that owns and operates physical network infrastructure — spectrum licenses, tower equipment, and core network. The distinction from MVNO: MNOs bear all capex and spectrum costs but control the network quality and economics. T-Mobile, AT&T, and Verizon are the three US MNOs at national scale. Dish/EchoStar is attempting to become the fourth via Open RAN deployment.

Common Workflows
Core operational processes — from activating a new subscriber to deploying a cell site and closing an enterprise deal.

New Subscriber Onboarding — Postpaid

How a new customer activates a postpaid line from store or digital channel through to a live, billing connection.
1
Plan & Device Selection
Customer selects plan and device online or in retail store. Promotional offers (trade-in credits, free phone tiers) are evaluated. Sales system checks current promos and applies device financing via EIP. Digital channel handles ~50% of new activations at major carriers.
2
Credit Check & Identity Verification
BSS system runs soft credit inquiry and identity verification. Fraud screening checks for synthetic identity and SIM swap risk. Credit score determines deposit requirement and EIP eligibility. Sub-prime customers may be offered prepaid alternatives.
3
SIM Provisioning
Physical SIM assigned or eSIM profile pushed to device. MSISDN (phone number) allocated from carrier number inventory. If porting from another carrier, local number portability (LNP) request initiated — completing within 2 hours per FCC rules. Losing carrier must release the number.
4
Network Registration
Device attaches to RAN, authenticates via HSS/UDM in core network. VoLTE profile provisioned for HD Voice. Data session established. Network management system records new subscriber against spectrum and geographic cell assignment.
5
Billing & Post-Activation Journey
BSS creates account with prorated first month billing. Autopay enrollment and paperless billing push reduce churn and care costs. Welcome SMS/email series triggers app download, feature discovery, and referral program introduction. Customer enters 90-day early-life churn risk window.

5G Macro Cell Site Build & Activation

How a carrier deploys a new 5G cell site from site acquisition through customer-facing go-live.
1
Site Acquisition
Real estate team negotiates lease with landowner or tower company (AMT, Crown Castle). Zoning permits obtained from municipality — often the longest-lead item (months to years in dense markets). Environmental and FAA reviews required for new tower builds. Colocation on existing towers accelerates timeline significantly.
2
RAN Equipment Installation
Ericsson, Nokia, or Samsung radio units and baseband hardware installed by tower crews. Fiber or microwave backhaul connection established to carrier transport network. Power systems (grid + backup generator) commissioned. Installation typically takes 1-3 days for a colocation upgrade; longer for new build.
3
Core Integration & Configuration
Site added to network management system. Licensed spectrum frequencies configured per carrier spectrum holdings and propagation plan. Neighbor cell relationships defined for handoff optimization. 5G NR parameters set for the band combination deployed at this site.
4
Drive Testing & RF Optimization
RF engineers perform drive testing to validate coverage, throughput, and interference. Parameter tuning adjusts antenna tilt, power levels, and handoff thresholds. OSS automated tools flag anomalies and suggest optimization. Acceptance testing verifies KPIs meet deployment standards before commercial launch.
5
Go-Live & Coverage Map Update
Site activated on production network. Coverage data integrated into carrier coverage map tool (customer-facing and internal). Network operations center monitors new site for alarms. Marketing may highlight coverage expansion in target geography. Customer-facing 5G speed improvement visible within days of activation.

Enterprise Private 5G Sale Cycle

How a carrier sells and deploys a private 5G network for an enterprise customer — warehouse, factory, or campus use case.
1
Discovery & Use Case Definition
Enterprise identifies connectivity need: warehouse automation, smart factory robotics, stadium Wi-Fi replacement, or campus IoT. Carrier and systems integrator partner conduct discovery workshops to map OT/IT requirements, latency needs, device density, and coverage footprint. CBRS vs licensed spectrum decision made based on enterprise location and requirements.
2
Solution Design
Carrier designs private network topology: small cell placement, spectrum allocation, core network (on-premise vs cloud), and integration with enterprise OT/IT systems. SLA targets defined (uptime, latency, throughput). Proposal includes hardware, software, managed service, and connectivity components. Systems integrator (Accenture, IBM, Ericsson) often co-sells.
3
Procurement & Legal
MSA (Master Services Agreement), SLA, and security review process initiated. Enterprise legal and IT security teams review data residency, access controls, and liability terms. Government and regulated enterprise customers require additional compliance review. Sales cycle typically 3-6 months for large enterprise. Proof-of-concept deployment may be required to close.
4
Deployment
Carrier deploys small cells on enterprise premises. Core network provisioned on-premise or in nearest carrier edge cloud location. Integration work connects private 5G to enterprise applications (WMS, MES, ERP). Device provisioning for robots, sensors, and employee handsets. Testing validates latency and throughput against SLA commitments.
5
Managed Service Handoff
Carrier network operations monitors uptime and QoS against SLA. Automated alerts escalate to carrier NOC for infrastructure issues. Enterprise IT manages applications layer independently. Quarterly business reviews cover SLA attainment and roadmap. Multi-year contract creates sticky recurring revenue with high switching costs.
Trends & Challenges
Forces reshaping the competitive, financial, and technological landscape of telecommunications.
💰
5G Monetization Lag
5G infrastructure is largely built across the US but monetization beyond premium tier pricing remains unclear for consumer use cases. Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) is the current 5G monetization winner. Enterprise private 5G is growing but slowly. Carrier ROI on $80B+ of 5G capex is under investor scrutiny as consumer ARPU growth stalls.
🏠
Fixed Wireless Access Disruption
T-Mobile Home Internet has surpassed 6M subscribers and continues to grow rapidly at $50/month — cheaper than most cable plans. Cable companies are losing broadband share for the first time in their history. FWA is enabled by excess mid-band 5G capacity and carries near 100% gross margin on incremental traffic. Could reshape the $100B cable broadband market over the next decade.
📉
Network Commoditization & Price Wars
Unlimited plans commoditized wireless — ARPU growth is challenged as carriers compete primarily on promotional device offers and streaming bundle giveaways (Netflix, Apple TV+). Churn is at historic lows but driven by installment lock-in, not loyalty. Cable company MVNOs (Comcast Xfinity Mobile, Charter Spectrum Mobile) are adding wireless subscribers at low CAC leveraging existing customer relationships.
🔧
Open RAN & Vendor Diversification
Dish/EchoStar built the first major commercial Open RAN network on AWS infrastructure. T-Mobile and AT&T are piloting O-RAN to reduce dependence on the Ericsson/Nokia duopoly. Software-defined RAN enables cloud-native automation and AI-driven optimization but integration complexity and performance parity with traditional RAN remain challenges. Geopolitical pressure accelerates vendor diversification.
📡
Spectrum Scarcity & Auction Economics
The C-band auction cost $81B in 2021. Future auctions — CBRS, 3.45GHz, 12GHz — are politically contested, with FCC authorization disputes delaying timelines. Spectrum is the single most critical long-term competitive asset. T-Mobile mid-band spectrum advantage from the Sprint merger is structural and compounds over time as 5G demand grows.
🛸
Satellite & Direct-to-Device
T-Mobile x SpaceX enables satellite coverage on standard unmodified smartphones — eliminating dead zones. AST SpaceMobile is building broadband LEO satellite capacity for direct mobile service. Starlink direct-to-cell service is commercially launching. This fundamentally redefines "coverage" as a differentiator vs. infrastructure build — coverage dead zones may disappear as a competitive axis within 5 years.
Market Dynamics & Monetization
Pain points, monetization models, and competitive forces shaping telecom.
Pain Points
Structural challenges facing carriers, operators, and the broader telecom ecosystem.

Customer Acquisition Cost

CAC for a postpaid subscriber is $300-500+. Intense promotional spend on device subsidies — "free phone" offers effectively spread $800+ device costs across 24-month installment credits. Cable companies (Comcast Xfinity Mobile, Charter Spectrum Mobile) entering wireless via MVNO increase competitive pressure with low-cost acquisition against existing broadband customers.

Churn & Switching Friction

eSIM reduces physical friction in carrier switching. Number portability is seamless under FCC rules. T-Mobile built market share by targeting AT&T and Verizon switchers with promotional offers. Network quality parity across carriers continues to reduce the technical switching barrier — differentiation increasingly depends on price and promotion rather than coverage or reliability.

Capex Treadmill

5G requires continuous spectrum investment and network densification. Carriers spend 15-20% of revenue on capex annually. Tower lease costs escalate at 3-4% per year under long-term lease agreements. Return on 5G consumer use case investment remains uncertain. FWA is the first clear 5G ROI story, but is limited by spectrum availability in dense markets.

Regulatory & Spectrum Constraints

FCC controls spectrum auctions; Congressional authorization required for new auction authority. Merger approvals are lengthy — T-Mobile/Sprint took 2 years and required divestitures. Net neutrality is a political pendulum shifting with administrations. FirstNet (AT&T government first responder network) represents both government contract opportunity and performance obligation risk.

ARPU Pressure

Unlimited plans removed the overage revenue that supplemented ARPU during the 4G era. Streaming bundle giveaways (Netflix, Hulu, Apple TV+, Disney+) embedded in premium plans erode effective margin. B2B and enterprise growth is needed to offset consumer commoditization. ARPU growth, when it occurs, requires market-wide pricing discipline from all three carriers simultaneously.

Market Structure: US wireless is a textbook oligopoly — T-Mobile, AT&T, and Verizon control approximately 99% of postpaid subscribers. Barriers to entry are extreme: spectrum costs $10B+, national network build exceeds $20B, and brand establishment requires years of promotional investment. Competition within the oligopoly is intense on promotion and price, but structural consolidation pressure remains as all three carriers seek scale economies. Cable company MVNO entry (Comcast, Charter) is the primary structural competitive change of the past decade — adding genuine price competition without network investment.
Evolution & Foundations
From Alexander Graham Bell to 5G — how telephone monopolies, spectrum auctions, the AT&T breakup, and the smartphone era transformed telecommunications into the infrastructure layer of the modern digital economy.
Foundational Works

Historical Timeline
1876–1906
Bell and the Monopoly Seed
Alexander Graham Bell patents the telephone (1876); Bell Telephone Company founded (1877); AT&T formed (1885) to build long-distance network; original Bell patents expire (1893–1894) — 6,000 independent phone companies form; AT&T acquires or undercuts competitors; Theodore Vail's "one system, one policy, universal service" doctrine cements vertical integration model that defines US telecom for a century.
1907–1984
The Bell System Era
AT&T reconstituted as regulated national monopoly; Kingsbury Commitment (1913) — AT&T agrees to government oversight to avoid antitrust breakup; Bell Labs founded (1925) — invents transistor (1947), solar cell, Unix, C language; regulated rate-of-return utility model; long-distance profit cross-subsidizes local service; 1956 consent decree limits AT&T to telecom; Carterphone decision (1968) opens terminal equipment market.
1984
The AT&T Breakup
DOJ antitrust suit leads to Modified Final Judgment (MFJ, 1982) — AT&T divests 22 local Bell Operating Companies into 7 Regional Bell Operating Companies (RBOCs) effective January 1, 1984; AT&T retains long-distance, Bell Labs, and Western Electric; MCI and Sprint enter long-distance competition; the biggest corporate restructuring in history reshapes US telecom competitive structure and indirectly creates conditions for wireless competition.
1983–2000
1G to 3G: Wireless Emerges
AMPS (Advanced Mobile Phone System) — first US cellular network (1983); DynaTAC 8000X first commercial cell phone ($3,995); digital cellular standards war: GSM (Europe/T-Mobile) vs. CDMA (Qualcomm/Verizon/Sprint); FCC spectrum auctions begin (1994) — PCS licenses raise $7B+; SMS launches commercially (1993); mobile subscribers surpass landlines globally (2002); T-Mobile US born from Deutsche Telekom's acquisition of VoiceStream (2001).
1996–2007
Telecom Act and Data Era
Telecommunications Act of 1996 — first major overhaul since 1934; opens local loop to competition; CLEC framework; enables RBOC mergers (SBC+AT&T 2005, Verizon+MCI 2005); 3G data networks (EVDO, HSDPA) enable mobile email; BlackBerry enterprise dominance; iPhone launch (June 2007) — defines the smartphone era; App Store (2008) transforms mobile from voice/SMS to platform; data traffic begins exponential growth trajectory.
2009–2013
4G LTE and the Un-carrier
LTE (Long Term Evolution) standard commercialized — Verizon first major US deployment (2010); AT&T, T-Mobile follow; data speeds 10x+ over 3G; streaming video becomes primary traffic; carrier subsidy model hides device cost in 2-year contracts; T-Mobile John Legere launches "Un-carrier" strategy (2013) — eliminates contracts, introduces EIP, offers free international roaming; T-Mobile Net Promoter Score surges; disrupts industry pricing assumptions.
2018–2020
5G and the Sprint Merger
3GPP Release 15 finalizes 5G NR (New Radio) standard (2018); T-Mobile, AT&T, Verizon launch 5G (2019–2020); T-Mobile/Sprint merger approved (April 2020) — creates 3-carrier national market; T-Mobile gains Sprint's 2.5 GHz mid-band spectrum (critical for 5G coverage+capacity); C-band auction raises $81B (2021); FWA (Fixed Wireless Access) launches as cable alternative; T-Mobile FWA reaches 5M customers by 2023.
2021–Present
Network Maturity and AI Integration
T-Mobile surpasses Verizon and AT&T in 5G coverage and speed rankings (2022–2024); Open RAN ecosystem grows as alternative to Ericsson/Nokia/Huawei; private 5G networks for enterprise campuses and factories; AI-driven network optimization (self-organizing networks, predictive maintenance); Fixed Wireless Access disrupts cable with 35M+ potential households addressable; 6G research begins; spectrum scarcity drives CBRS (3.5 GHz) shared access model and mmWave urban densification.

Data Governance & Regulatory Notes
🔒

CPNI — Customer Proprietary Network Information

FCC rules restrict how carriers use call metadata (who called whom, when, from where). CPNI cannot be shared with third parties without consent. Annual CPNI compliance certifications required. Carriers have faced $100M+ FCC fines for selling location data to data brokers.

🔒

CALEA — Lawful Intercept

US law requires all carriers to build wiretap capabilities into their networks. Law enforcement can compel real-time intercept. Data retention requirements vary by jurisdiction (EU Data Retention Directive struck down; GDPR now governs). 5G SA introduces new CALEA complexity with cloud-native functions.

🔒

SS7 / Diameter Security Reporting

CISA and FCC now require carriers to report SS7/Diameter abuse incidents. Despite known vulnerabilities since 2008, SS7 is still in active use globally. Carriers have legal obligations to protect CPNI against SS7 attacks.

🔒

Net Neutrality & Traffic Data

Rules on traffic management (throttling, zero-rating) require carriers to maintain records of QoS treatment by application/class. In jurisdictions with strong net neutrality, DPI-based traffic management data is subject to regulatory scrutiny.

Failure Modes & Incidents
High-profile incidents that reshaped how this industry thinks about data, security, and operational resilience. Each reveals a systemic assumption that broke under stress.
📱

T-Mobile Data Breach — 76 Million Records (August 2021)

An attacker accessed T-Mobile's systems via an unprotected API exposed on a testing server, exfiltrating SSNs, driver's licenses, IMEI numbers, and account PINs for 76 million current and former customers. T-Mobile's third major breach in three years.

Root cause: Revealed: telecom carriers hold extraordinarily sensitive identity data (SSN + device + account) but API security and network exposure management lagged the value of what was being protected.

Lesson: $350M class-action settlement; mandatory CISA-reviewed security plan. Zero-trust API gateway with auth tokens required; external attack surface management is non-negotiable.

🔓

SS7 Protocol Exploitation (documented from 2014, ongoing)

Security researchers demonstrated at CCC 2014 that SS7 — the 1970s signaling protocol underpinning global telephony — allows any telecom operator (or attacker with SS7 access) to track any phone's location globally, intercept SMS (including 2FA codes), and forward calls, regardless of encryption.

Root cause: Revealed: the global phone system has an unfixable architectural flaw. Intelligence agencies and criminal groups have exploited SS7 for years. Banks using SMS 2FA are particularly vulnerable.

Lesson: GSMA FS.11 monitoring guidance; carriers must deploy SS7 firewalls; 2FA systems must migrate from SMS to TOTP/FIDO2; migration to DIAMETER (4G) and SBA (5G) reduces but does not eliminate risk.

🌐

CenturyLink BGP Misconfiguration — 911 Outages (August 2020)

A BGP routing table error at CenturyLink/Lumen cascaded across the internet for hours, taking down 911 emergency services in multiple US states, disrupting hospitals, and affecting millions of internet users across multiple carriers.

Root cause: Revealed: telecom BGP routing has critical single points of failure. A single carrier's misconfiguration can cascade to 911 services — infrastructure with direct life-safety implications.

Lesson: RPKI (Route Origin Validation) adoption; BGP route change rate limiting; 911 traffic must have dedicated, protected routing separate from general internet traffic.

🔐

SIM Swap Attacks via Telecom Insiders (AT&T, T-Mobile, Verizon — multiple cases, 2018–2022)

Employees at major US carriers were bribed or socially engineered to perform unauthorized SIM swaps, enabling cryptocurrency theft from high-value targets. One employee facilitated $5M+ in losses. Telecom insiders have direct access to the SIM reassignment systems.

Root cause: Revealed: SIM swap is a carrier-side attack that bypasses device security entirely. Gig economy support staff have the same SIM management access as full-time employees.

Lesson: Multi-factor authentication for SIM swap requests (biometric + PIN + in-person for high-risk accounts); insider threat monitoring on SIM management system access; number lock / port freeze products.

🇦🇺

Optus Network Outage — 10 Million Customers (November 2023)

A BGP routing update from a software upgrade cascaded into a full Optus network outage lasting up to 14 hours for 10 million customers. Triple Zero (000) emergency calls were affected. Root cause: a BGP route change triggered a mismatch in Cisco routers causing cascading resets.

Root cause: Revealed: a single software upgrade in a carrier core network can take down national emergency services without adequate circuit breakers or staged rollout controls.

Lesson: Mandatory staged rollout of network software updates; BGP change circuit breakers; 000/911 traffic must route via independent paths with automatic failover to alternate carriers.

📡

Carrier Location Data Sales to Data Brokers (FCC Investigation, 2018–2020)

It was revealed that all four major US carriers (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile, Sprint) were selling real-time customer location data to data brokers and law enforcement without court orders, violating CPNI rules. FCC proposed $200M in fines.

Root cause: Revealed: CPNI regulations were not preventing systematic monetization of sensitive location data. Consent mechanisms were buried in terms of service. Self-policing of carrier data practices is ineffective without strong enforcement.

Lesson: $200M FCC fines finalized in 2024. Location data sharing must require explicit opt-in and real-time customer visibility; data broker contracts must be published; annual CPNI audits must include third-party data flows.
