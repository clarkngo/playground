What the Industry Does

E-commerce platforms connect buyers and sellers at scale, handling product discovery, transactions, logistics, and payments. The two dominant models are first-party (1P) retail — where the platform buys and resells inventory — and third-party (3P) marketplaces — where the platform facilitates transactions between independent sellers and buyers, earning a take rate on each sale. Supply chain and fulfillment operations are critical differentiators.

Key Terminology

Search or click any term to expand its definition.

GMVGross Merchandise Value
▶
Total dollar value of all transactions on the platform before deducting fees, returns, or cancellations. The primary top-line metric for marketplace health. Does not equal revenue — a platform with $100B GMV may earn only $15B in revenue after take rates. business
Take Rate
▶
The percentage of GMV retained by the platform as revenue. Includes listing fees, referral fees, payment processing, and fulfillment margins. Typical range: 6–20% for consumer marketplaces. business
AOVAverage Order Value
▶
Total revenue divided by number of orders over a period. Higher AOV reduces per-order fulfillment cost as a percentage. Driven up by upsell, bundles, and free shipping thresholds. business
Cart Abandonment Rate
▶
Percentage of shoppers who add items to cart but do not complete purchase. Industry average is ~70%. Caused by unexpected shipping costs, forced account creation, complex checkout, or price comparison behavior. conversion
CACCustomer Acquisition Cost
▶
Total marketing and sales spend divided by new customers acquired. Sustainable businesses require LTV > 3x CAC. CAC has risen significantly as iOS privacy changes degraded ad targeting efficiency. marketing
LTV / CLVCustomer Lifetime Value
▶
Predicted net revenue from a customer over their entire relationship. Calculated as: (AOV × Purchase Frequency × Gross Margin) / Churn Rate. The most important long-term unit economic metric. marketing
ROASReturn on Ad Spend
▶
Revenue generated per dollar of advertising spend. ROAS = Revenue / Ad Spend. A ROAS of 4x means $4 revenue for every $1 spent. Must exceed gross margin threshold to be profitable. marketing
SKUStock Keeping Unit
▶
A unique identifier for a product variant (e.g., Blue T-Shirt, Size M). Distinct from UPC — one product may have many SKUs for different sizes/colors. Catalog management systems track inventory at the SKU level. catalog
UPCUniversal Product Code
▶
Industry-standard 12-digit barcode identifier assigned by GS1. Used for physical scanning and cross-platform product matching. Enables catalog deduplication and price comparison. catalog
ASINAmazon Standard Identification Number
▶
Amazon-specific 10-character alphanumeric ID assigned to every product in their catalog. Multiple sellers can list on the same ASIN, competing for the Buy Box. Controlling the canonical ASIN is strategically valuable. catalog
FBAFulfillment by Amazon
▶
Sellers ship inventory to Amazon warehouses; Amazon handles picking, packing, shipping, and returns. FBA products qualify for Prime. Amazon charges fulfillment fees per unit plus monthly storage fees. fulfillment
3PLThird-Party Logistics
▶
Outsourced logistics providers handling warehousing, picking, packing, and shipping. ShipBob, ShipMonk, and Flexport are leading 3PLs. Allows brands to scale without owning physical infrastructure. fulfillment
Last-Mile Delivery
▶
The final leg of shipping from a local hub to the customer doorstep. The most expensive and complex part of logistics — accounts for 50%+ of total delivery cost. Key battleground for same-day and next-day delivery. fulfillment
Cross-Docking
▶
A fulfillment technique where inbound shipments are directly transferred to outbound trucks with minimal warehousing time. Reduces inventory holding costs and speeds delivery. Used heavily in grocery and perishables. fulfillment
Dropshipping
▶
Retailer sells products without holding inventory. When an order is placed, the retailer purchases from a supplier who ships directly to the customer. Low capital but poor shipping times and quality control. fulfillment
BM25Best Match 25
▶
Probabilistic ranking function used in full-text search. Extension of TF-IDF that accounts for document length and term saturation. Default algorithm in Elasticsearch/OpenSearch. Still the baseline retrieval method for product search. search
Learning-to-RankL2R / LTR
▶
ML approach to reranking search results using features like click-through rate, conversion rate, price, availability, and seller rating. LambdaMART and neural re-rankers are common. Requires massive labeled click data to train. search
Semantic Search
▶
Search using dense vector embeddings (BERT, sentence transformers) rather than exact keyword matching. Understands intent and synonyms. Combined with BM25 in hybrid retrieval (sparse + dense) for best results. search
CNP FraudCard Not Present
▶
Credit card fraud occurring in online transactions where the physical card is not used. The dominant fraud type in e-commerce. Detected via velocity checks, device fingerprinting, billing/shipping address mismatch, and ML models. fraud
Chargeback
▶
When a cardholder disputes a charge with their bank, forcing a reversal. Merchants pay chargeback fees ($15–$100 each). High chargeback rates (>1%) can result in losing payment processing. First-party fraud (friendly fraud) is rising. fraud
Device Fingerprinting
▶
Identifying a device via browser attributes (user agent, screen resolution, fonts, WebGL, canvas fingerprint) without cookies. Used in fraud detection to link fraudulent accounts and detect emulators or headless browsers. fraud
Dynamic Pricing
▶
Algorithmic real-time price adjustment based on supply, demand, competitor prices, and customer segment. Used by Amazon (millions of price changes daily), airlines, hotels, and ride-sharing. Requires demand forecasting models. pricing
Buy BoxFeatured Offer
▶
The default seller selected for a given ASIN when a buyer clicks Add to Cart. Determined by price, fulfillment speed, seller rating, and FBA status. ~80% of Amazon sales go through the Buy Box winner. pricing
MAPMinimum Advertised Price
▶
Brand-set floor price below which resellers cannot advertise a product. Not legally enforceable in all jurisdictions, but brands enforce via distribution agreements. Protects brand equity and dealer margins. pricing
Collaborative Filtering
▶
Recommends items based on patterns of similar users (user-user) or similar items (item-item). Foundation of Amazon and Netflix recommendations. Relies on implicit feedback (clicks, purchases, dwell time) rather than explicit ratings. recommendations
Implicit Feedback
▶
User behavior signals (clicks, add-to-cart, purchases, dwell time, scrolls) used as training data for recommendation models. More abundant than explicit ratings (1–5 stars) but noisier and harder to interpret. recommendations
Embeddings
▶
Dense vector representations of items, queries, or users in a latent space. Items close in embedding space are semantically similar. Used in two-tower retrieval models for recommendations and neural search. recommendations
Price Elasticity
▶
Measure of how sensitive demand is to price changes. Elasticity = % change in quantity / % change in price. Elastic goods (>1) see large demand drops with price increases. Essential input for dynamic pricing optimization. pricing

Technology Stack

The key systems and ML components powering modern e-commerce platforms.

Search & Retrieval

Elasticsearch OpenSearch Solr Vespa FAISS (ANN) ScaNN

BM25 for sparse retrieval + dense vector ANN for semantic recall. Neural re-ranking uses BERT/T5 cross-encoders. Query understanding handles spelling, expansion, and intent classification.

Recommendations

ALS (Implicit) BPR Two-Tower Neural Item2Vec SASRec TensorFlow Serving

Two-tower models learn user and item embeddings jointly. Real-time serving via approximate nearest neighbor lookup. Candidate generation + ranking pipeline similar to search. Session-based models capture short-term intent.

Pricing & Inventory

Rule Engines XGBoost Demand Forecast Prophet LP Solvers (Gurobi) RL Pricing Agents

Demand forecasting combines time-series models with external signals (weather, events). Dynamic pricing optimization balances revenue, margin, and competitiveness. Safety stock and reorder point calculations feed inventory management systems.

Fraud Detection

Graph Neural Networks Feast (Feature Store) Apache Flink XGBoost LightGBM Device Fingerprint APIs

Graph models detect fraud rings by analyzing account relationship networks. Real-time feature stores (Feast, Tecton) serve low-latency features for online scoring. Stream processing (Flink/Spark Streaming) computes velocity features in real time.

Infrastructure

Apache Kafka Redis Cassandra DynamoDB PostgreSQL Snowflake Kubernetes

Kafka handles event streaming for order events, inventory updates, and click streams. Redis serves session state, cart, and recommendation caches. Cassandra/DynamoDB for high-write catalog and inventory data. Snowflake or BigQuery for analytics.

A/B Testing & Experimentation

Optimizely LaunchDarkly Internal Platforms Statsig Sequential Testing

Feature flags enable gradual rollout. Interleaving experiments allow fast ranking A/B tests. CUPED variance reduction reduces sample size requirements. Switchback experiments handle marketplace interference effects.

ML Platform

MLflow Vertex AI SageMaker Feast Tecton Airflow dbt

Feature stores bridge offline training and online serving. Model registry tracks versions and metrics. Online/offline evaluation pipelines compare ranking quality. Shadow scoring validates new models before production traffic.

Trends & Challenges

Forces reshaping e-commerce over the next 3–5 years.

🤖

AI-Personalized Shopping

Large language models enable conversational search and 1:1 real-time personalization at scale. LLM-powered shopping assistants (Amazon Rufus, Perplexity Shopping) can interpret natural language queries, compare products, and generate personalized recommendations based on purchase history and stated preferences.

📲

Social Commerce

TikTok Shop, Instagram Checkout, and Pinterest Shopping blur the boundary between content and commerce. Live-stream selling is exploding in Southeast Asia and growing in the US. Creator storefronts and shoppable video reduce the steps from inspiration to purchase.

⚡

Quick Commerce

Sub-30-minute delivery via dark stores (micro-fulfillment centers in urban areas). Gopuff, Instacart, and DoorDash Dash Mart compete on speed. Requires dense inventory positioning, tight delivery radius management, and sophisticated real-time routing algorithms.

🛡️

Marketplace Integrity

AI-powered fake review detection, counterfeit listing identification, and fraud ring disruption. As AI makes review generation cheaper, platforms invest in LLM-based review authenticity scoring, seller identity graphs, and coordinated inauthentic behavior detection.

🏭

Supply Chain Resilience

Post-pandemic near-shoring and supply chain diversification. Demand for real-time supply chain visibility platforms (Flexport, project44). ML demand planning reduces bullwhip effect. Geopolitical risk modeling becomes a core competency for large retailers.

♻️

Recommerce & Sustainability

eBay Recommerce, ThredUp, Poshmark, and brand-owned resale programs respond to Gen Z preference for sustainability. Circular economy mandates emerging in the EU. Luxury brands launch certified pre-owned programs to capture secondary market value and fight counterfeiting.
