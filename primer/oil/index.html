Industry Overview
Oil and gas is the backbone of global energy — spanning exploration and production (E&P) in remote basins, pipeline networks moving hydrocarbons across continents, and refineries converting crude into fuels and petrochemicals. The industry generates trillions in annual revenue while navigating the largest structural energy transition in a century.
⛏️

Upstream / E&P

Exploration and Production: finding and extracting oil and gas. ExxonMobil, Chevron, ConocoPhillips, Pioneer, Devon. Onshore (shale/tight oil) and offshore (deepwater). Capital-intensive; reserve replacement is existential metric.

🛢️

Midstream

Pipelines, storage terminals, LNG export facilities. Kinder Morgan, Energy Transfer, Williams Companies. Toll-road model — fees per volume transported. Essential infrastructure with limited competition; regulated in most jurisdictions.

🏭

Downstream / Refining & Petrochemicals

Crude oil refined into gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, plastics feedstocks. Phillips 66, Valero, Marathon Petroleum. Refining margin (crack spread) is key profitability driver. Petrochemicals (ethylene, propylene) are growing vs motor fuels.

🇸🇦

National Oil Companies / NOCs

Saudi Aramco, ADNOC, NOC Libya, PEMEX, Petrobras. State-owned; control 70%+ of global reserves. Political as well as commercial objectives. Often subsidy-driven pricing.

🔧

Oilfield Services

Schlumberger (SLB), Halliburton, Baker Hughes. Drilling, completion, logging, well services. Essential to E&P. Pricing power tied to rig count and upstream capex cycles.

🚢

LNG & Natural Gas

Liquefied Natural Gas: chilling gas to -162C for transport. Qatar, Australia, US (Sabine Pass, Freeport) as top LNG exporters. Europe shifted from Russian pipeline gas to LNG post-2022 invasion. US LNG exports $34B in 2023.

$60-90/bbl range supports stable investment cycle. Below $50 triggers E&P capex cuts; above $100 accelerates demand destruction and energy transition investment.

OPEC+

Saudi Arabia and Russia coordinate production quotas controlling ~40% of global supply. Production cuts can spike prices $10-20/bbl within weeks of announcement.

US Shale Revolution

Horizontal drilling and fracking made the US the world largest oil producer at 13M bbl/day. Permian Basin alone exceeds 6M bbl/day output.

Saudi Aramco

Most profitable company in history; $161B net income in 2022. Lifting cost ~$2.50/bbl but national budget requires ~$80/bbl. IPO in 2019 raised $25.6B.

Energy Transition Pressure

Permian Basin

Most productive US shale basin; 6M+ bbl/day. Midland and Delaware sub-basins. ExxonMobil Pioneer acquisition 2024 consolidates premier Permian acreage.

Key Terminology
Essential vocabulary for oil and gas — upstream economics, trading, refining margins, and energy policy concepts.
Brent / WTIPricing▼
Benchmark crude oil prices. Brent (North Sea) is the global benchmark used for ~70% of internationally traded crude. WTI (West Texas Intermediate) is the US benchmark traded on CME NYMEX. Typical Brent-WTI spread is $2-5/bbl, driven by logistics and quality differences.
Barrel (bbl)Core▼
Standard unit of oil volume — 42 US gallons. Most pricing and production quoted in barrels per day (bbl/d or bpd). Global consumption is approximately 103 million bpd. BOE (barrels of oil equivalent) converts gas volumes to oil equivalents for reporting.
Upstream / Midstream / DownstreamCore▼
Three segments of the oil and gas industry. Upstream = E&P (exploration and production). Midstream = transportation and storage infrastructure. Downstream = refining, petrochemicals, and marketing. Integrated majors (ExxonMobil, Shell) operate across all three; most companies specialize in one or two.
Proved Reserves (1P)E&P▼
Oil and gas quantities with 90% certainty extractable under current economic conditions. Critical valuation metric for E&P companies. SEC requires annual reserve certifications. 2P = probable reserves (50% certainty); 3P = possible (10% certainty). Reserve life index = reserves / annual production.
Reserve Replacement Ratio (RRR)E&P▼
New reserves added divided by production volume. RRR above 100% means a company is growing its reserve base; below 100% means it is depleting its asset base faster than it is replacing. A key existential metric for E&P companies. Major shale companies often run RRR of 150-300% during growth phases.
Lifting Cost / Production CostE&P▼
Cost to produce one barrel from an existing well. US shale ~$20-40/bbl lifting cost; Saudi Aramco ~$2.50/bbl lifting cost but $80+ needed for national budget balance. Lifting cost excludes finding and development costs (F&D) and is a measure of operational efficiency on producing wells.
Breakeven PriceE&P▼
Oil price at which a project or company is cash-flow neutral. US shale projects: $35-55 WTI; deepwater offshore: $40-65/bbl. Fiscal breakeven (OPEC nations): Saudi Arabia ~$80, Russia ~$65, Iraq ~$50. Guides OPEC quota decisions. Permian Tier 1 wells have among the lowest breakevens globally.
Crack SpreadRefining▼
Refining margin — spread between crude input cost and refined product output prices. Gulf Coast 3-2-1 crack spread is the benchmark: 3 bbl crude yields 2 bbl gasoline + 1 bbl diesel. Normal crack spread $15-30/bbl; spiked above $60/bbl in mid-2022 following Russian invasion disruption. The primary profitability driver for refiners.
Fracking / Hydraulic FracturingTechnology▼
High-pressure fluid injection to fracture tight rock and release trapped oil and gas. Enabled the US shale boom — production grew from 5M to 13M bpd between 2008 and 2023. Controversial due to water use (5-10M gallons per well), seismic risk from produced water disposal, and methane emissions. Combined with horizontal drilling reaching 10,000+ ft laterals.
OPEC+ (Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries + Russia)Markets▼
Cartel coordinating production quotas. Saudi Arabia is de facto leader; Russia joined after 2016 to form OPEC+. Controls roughly 40% of global supply. Production cuts can spike prices $10-20/bbl within weeks. Quota discipline is the core challenge — smaller members (UAE, Iraq) regularly produce above quota. US shale acts as a structural cap on price rallies.
Henry HubPricing▼
Benchmark US natural gas price. Spot and futures pricing at the Erath, Louisiana pipeline hub. Quoted in $/MMBtu (million British thermal units). Volatile: ranged from $1.63 (2020) to $9.68/MMBtu (2022). Global LNG contracts often priced at Henry Hub + tolling fee. European benchmark is TTF (Title Transfer Facility, Netherlands).
LNG (Liquefied Natural Gas)Core▼
Natural gas cooled to -162C and liquefied for tanker transport. Enables global gas trade where pipelines are not feasible. Major exporters: Qatar, Australia, and US (Sabine Pass, Freeport, Corpus Christi). Requires liquefaction terminals at export end and regasification terminals at destination. Post-2022 European energy crisis drove massive LNG demand growth.
Wellbore / CompletionDrilling▼
Wellbore = the drilled hole. Completion = perforating, fracking, and equipping the well for production. Completion costs are the majority of a shale well total cost ($4-8M for completion vs $2-4M for drilling). A Permian well may have 80-120 frac stages along a 10,000+ ft lateral. Completion intensity (proppant per foot) is a key performance driver.
API GravityCore▼
Measure of crude oil density relative to water. Higher API = lighter crude = more gasoline yield with less complex refining. WTI ~39 API (sweet light crude). Brent ~38 API. Heavy crude (Orinoco: 8 API, Canadian oil sands: 8-12 API) requires more energy-intensive and costly refining. Sweet crude = low sulfur; sour = high sulfur.
Wet vs Dry GasE&P▼
Dry gas = mostly methane (heating and power generation). Wet gas = methane plus NGLs (natural gas liquids: ethane, propane, butane, pentane). NGLs are valuable petrochemical feedstocks. Marcellus wet window produces significant ethane for ethylene crackers. NGL prices are partially decoupled from gas prices — adding revenue optionality to wet gas producers.
EUR (Estimated Ultimate Recovery)E&P▼
Total expected production from a well over its lifetime. Key input for shale well economics. Permian Tier 1 wells: EUR of 600K-1.2M BOE. Production decline curves are steep in shale (50-70% first year) then flatten. EUR times net revenue interest times commodity price minus total well cost determines well economics.
Day RateOilfield Services▼
Daily rental cost for a drilling rig. Offshore deepwater semi-submersible or drillship: $300K-$500K/day at peak. Offshore jackup rigs: $80K-$150K/day. Onshore land rigs: $20K-50K/day. Day rates are a proxy for upstream activity level and OFS company revenue. Collapsed to near zero for some rigs during COVID 2020.
NetbackE&P▼
Wellhead revenue minus transport, processing, and royalty costs. The true realized price per barrel at the wellhead. Canadian oil sands producers often face $15-25/bbl netback discounts due to landlocked position and heavy oil differentials. Permian producers with Corpus Christi access capture better netbacks than those without Gulf Coast egress.
Carbon IntensityESG▼
CO2 equivalent per barrel of oil equivalent (BOE) produced. Growing ESG disclosure requirement from investors and regulators. Lower = better. Equinor and BP report among lower carbon intensity upstream portfolios. Flaring (burning associated gas) and methane venting are the largest contributors. SEC climate disclosure rules now require Scope 1 and 2 reporting for large accelerated filers.
Royalty / Working InterestE&P▼
Royalty owner receives a percentage of gross revenue with no cost obligation (typically 12.5-25% of production value). Working interest owner funds drilling and completion costs in exchange for production revenue share. Mineral rights ownership in the US is severable from surface rights, creating a large market for mineral rights acquisition and aggregation.

Shale Well Drilling & Completion (Permian Basin)

How an oil company drills and brings a horizontal shale well to production — from land acquisition to first oil sales.
1
Land Acquisition & Permitting
Lease mineral rights from landowner (typically 1/8 to 1/4 royalty); secure drilling permit from state regulator (TX RRC for Texas); engineer well pad design for multi-well development. Pad drilling allows 4-12 wells from single surface location to minimize footprint.
2
Drill Vertical + Curve + Lateral
10,000+ ft lateral drilled after kickoff point. MWD (Measurement While Drilling) and LWD (Logging While Drilling) tools provide real-time geology data. Target landing zone within 50-ft window in the Wolfcamp or Spraberry formation. Total well depth 14,000-18,000 ft measured depth.
3
Completion Design & Execution
Select perforation clusters, frac fluid recipe (slickwater + proppant), and proppant loading (lbs per foot). Pump 80-120 frac stages per well. $4-8M completion cost using 1,500-2,500 lb/ft of proppant. Completion design is the primary driver of well performance differentiation.
4
Flowback & Early Production
Well flows back frac fluid and formation water (produced water). Water disposed via SWD (salt water disposal) well injection. First oil sales begin within days of flowback; IP30 rate measured over first month. Gas is gathered and sold or flared (regulatory limits apply).
5
Production Monitoring & Lift Optimization
Wellsite sensors stream data to SCADA and historian (OSIsoft PI). AI models predict decline curves and flag optimization opportunities. Artificial lift (ESP pumps or rod pumps) installed as reservoir pressure declines. EUR and breakeven tracked against actual production to refine future well designs.

Crude Oil Trading & Physical Delivery

How a crude oil cargo is priced, purchased, shipped, and settled between producer and refinery.
1
Price Discovery
Trader monitors Brent/WTI futures on CME NYMEX and ICE. Physical differentials priced via Argus and Platts (S&P Global) daily assessments. Dated Brent (D-Brent) is the physical benchmark for North Sea cargoes. Spread between futures and physical drives arbitrage opportunities.
2
Cargo Nomination
Producer nominates cargo for loading month+1. Buyer confirms vessel type and loading date. BFOET (Brent, Forties, Oseberg, Ekofisk, Troll) assessment process sets Dated Brent pricing benchmark. Term contracts (monthly, annual) negotiated separately from spot market.
3
Vessel Chartering
VLCC (Very Large Crude Carrier, 2M bbl) or Aframax (750K bbl) chartered via shipbroker. Charterparty (charter agreement) signed; day rate negotiated based on Baltic Exchange tanker index. AIS (Automatic Identification System) tracking confirms vessel position.
4
Loading & Documentation
Terminal allocates tank and berth; loading quantity determined by shore tank gauging and independent inspector. Bill of Lading (B/L) issued as title document. Certificate of Quality confirms API gravity, sulfur content, and other specifications. Ullage report documents tank measurements.
5
Settlement & Delivery
Price final based on load date pricing (Dated Brent +/- differential). Payment via letter of credit (L/C) or wire transfer at T+30 from B/L date. Cargo tracked via AIS to destination refinery. Arrival inspection at discharge port confirms quantity and quality.

Refinery Crude Procurement & Scheduling

How a refinery optimizes its crude diet, purchases feedstock, and schedules processing to maximize margin.
1
Crude Slate Optimization
Linear programming (LP) model optimizes crude mix based on yield targets, processing unit constraints, crude prices, and product margins. Aspen PIMS or Haverly GRTMPS are standard LP tools. Crude quality (API, sulfur, TAN) determines unit loading and product slate.
2
Crude Purchase & Hedging
Trader purchases crude 30-90 days forward on term or spot basis. Crude price risk hedged using NYMEX futures (WTI) or ICE Brent futures. Product cracks may also be hedged to lock in refining margin. Treasury approves hedge book exposure limits.
3
Vessel Discharge Scheduling
Tank farm capacity checked against expected arrivals. Crude rundown schedule set by planning team to maintain continuous CDU (crude distillation unit) feed. Vessel discharge rate, tank routing, and blending sequence determined by operations.
4
Processing & Unit Operations
CDU processes crude into atmospheric fractions (naphtha, kerosene, diesel, heavy gasoil, atmospheric residue). Downstream units convert heavy fractions: FCC (fluid catalytic cracker) and hydrocracker produce gasoline and diesel; coker processes vacuum residue into petroleum coke. Yield optimized against real-time product prices.
5
Product Disposition
Gasoline and diesel sold via rack (terminal loading) or pipeline to fuel distributors. Jet fuel sold to airlines under term supply agreements. Petrochemical feedstocks (naphtha, propylene) sold to crackers. Product inventory managed against spot vs term sales schedule.
Trends & Challenges
Forces reshaping oil and gas — from energy transition tension to US shale consolidation, methane regulation, and the digital oilfield.
🌱
Energy Transition vs Demand Reality
IEA scenarios require no new oil field approvals for 1.5C pathway. But global oil demand hit record 103M bpd in 2023. The Global South (India, Southeast Asia, Africa) is still driving growth. Majors investing in low-carbon while defending E&P cash flows. The "managed decline" vs continued investment tension defines boardroom strategy debates.
💰
US Shale Maturity & Consolidation
Permian Basin production exceeds 6M bpd but Tier-1 acreage inventory is being consumed. Exxon/Pioneer ($60B) and Chevron/Hess ($53B) megamergers signal consolidation phase. Private equity backing the earlier shale boom is exiting via public M&A. 100+ independents of 2015 consolidating to a handful of large operators.
🇸🇦
OPEC+ Cohesion & Market Management
Saudi Arabia and Russia managing price via production quotas. Quota cheating by smaller members (UAE, Iraq) weakens cartel discipline. US shale as swing producer complicates OPEC+ leverage. Saudi fiscal breakeven ~$80/bbl creates strong incentive to defend price. 2023 voluntary cuts pushed price to $90+ before demand concerns re-emerged.
💨
Methane Regulation & ESG Pressure
Methane is 80x more potent than CO2 at a 20-year horizon. EPA methane rules (2024) target upstream venting and flaring from existing sources. Institutional investors divesting from highest-emission producers. Scope 3 emissions disclosure expanding under SEC rules. Kayrros satellite monitoring making field-level methane emissions publicly visible.
🛢️
LNG as Bridge Fuel
Russia-Ukraine war made Europe a massive LNG importer virtually overnight. US LNG exports tripled 2019-2023. New US export terminals (Corpus Christi Stage 3, Plaquemines LNG) adding 30+ Mtpa capacity. Asia LNG demand growing. 20-year offtake contracts locking in gas infrastructure for decades — structural tension with net-zero timelines.
🤖
Digital Oilfield & AI Optimization
SLB Lumi AI platform targeting drilling automation and production optimization. ML-assisted seismic interpretation reducing cycle time from months to weeks. C3.ai oil production optimization deployed at major E&Ps. Digital twins for refineries improving yield and energy efficiency. Cybersecurity of OT/SCADA systems is a critical and under-resourced risk.
Market Dynamics & Monetization
Pain points, monetization models, and competitive forces in oil and gas.
Pain Points

Commodity Price Volatility

Oil prices swung from -$37/bbl (WTI April 2020) to $130/bbl (2022 post-Ukraine invasion). Impossible to forecast 3-5 years out. Capex decisions made on long-lead projects with 3-7 year payback periods. Volatility kills investment discipline and creates boom-bust cycles across the entire supply chain.

Permitting & Regulatory Uncertainty

US federal leasing moratoriums, state-level environmental reviews, and NEPA challenges delay pipeline projects 5-10 years. Dakota Access and Mountain Valley Pipeline faced multi-year legal battles. Regulatory uncertainty raises cost of capital for infrastructure investment. International: host government terms renegotiation risk for NOC-IOC joint ventures.

Energy Transition Asset Stranding Risk

$500B+ in existing upstream projects could become stranded assets under aggressive IEA net-zero scenarios. Investors pricing in transition risk via higher discount rates for oil projects. BP wrote down $17.5B in 2020. Impairment cycles can damage balance sheets and force production cuts at exactly the wrong time in the commodity cycle.

Workforce & Skill Gap

Petroleum engineers are retiring; younger workforce preferring clean energy careers. Drilling boom-bust cycles create feast-or-famine hiring that breaks institutional knowledge continuity. OFS companies carry high fixed labor costs through downturns. Digital and data science talent is the new scarcity — remote operations require software engineers, not just roughnecks.

Water Management in Shale

Permian shale produces 10+ barrels of water per barrel of oil. Produced water disposal via SWD (salt water disposal) well injection linked to seismic activity (Oklahoma earthquake surge). Water scarcity in arid West Texas basins. Treatment and recycling infrastructure costs $0.50-1.50/bbl. Recycling is growing but still minority of disposal volume.

Market Structure

Oligopoly at Top

OPEC+ NOCs and the 5 integrated majors control strategic assets and long-cycle production. Saudi Aramco alone controls reserves exceeding all US producers combined. Coordination via OPEC+ supply management is a structural feature.

Fragmented US Shale

100+ E&P companies compete in US unconventional. No single player controls more than 15% of Permian output. Competition is on cost structure, acreage quality, and capital efficiency — consolidation is rapidly reducing that number.

OFS Oligopoly

SLB, Halliburton, and Baker Hughes control the majority of drilling and completion services globally. Technology differentiation (automation, digital) is creating widening margins vs mid-tier OFS. Pricing power follows rig count with a 6-12 month lag.

Commodity Pricing

Unlike tech or pharma, differentiation in oil and gas is on cost structure and asset quality, not brand. A barrel of WTI from Pioneer is identical to one from Devon. Low-cost producers with superior acreage win in every price environment.

Capital Cycle Dynamics

Long lead times (3-7 years from FID to production for deepwater/LNG) mean supply response is slow. Shale is the exception — 6-12 month cycle time. This creates persistent price volatility: underinvestment in 2020 contributed to $130/bbl in 2022.

NOC vs IOC Competition

NOCs control access to lowest-cost reserves. IOCs compete on technology, capital, and execution. Production sharing agreements and concession terms determine how rent is split. Resource nationalism (renegotiating terms in rising price environments) is a persistent IOC risk.

Evolution & Foundations
How the oil and gas industry evolved from Drake's first well through the Standard Oil monopoly, OPEC formation, shale revolution, and today's energy transition tension — and the foundational works that shaped it.
1859

Drake Well & Modern Oil Industry

Edwin Drake drills first commercial oil well in Titusville, Pennsylvania (August 27, 1859); 69.5 feet deep; produces 25 barrels/day; triggers Pennsylvania oil rush; Standard Oil founded (John D. Rockefeller, 1870); kerosene replaces whale oil for lighting; modern petroleum industry born.

1882–1911

Standard Oil Monopoly & Breakup

Standard Oil controls 91% of US refining by 1882; trust structure invented to circumvent state anti-trust laws; Sherman Antitrust Act (1890); US Supreme Court breaks up Standard Oil (1911) into 34 companies including Jersey Standard (ExxonMobil), Socal (Chevron), Mobil; the Seven Sisters — major international oil companies — descend from Standard Oil.

1908–1940s

Middle East Discovery & IOCs

Gulf Oil discovers oil in Kuwait (1938); Aramco founded in Saudi Arabia (1944, by Chevron/Texaco/Exxon/Mobil); Iranian oil nationalization (Mosaddegh, 1951) and CIA-backed coup (1953); the power dynamic between IOCs and host governments established; oil as geopolitical instrument first deployed.

1960–1973

OPEC Formation & First Oil Crisis

OPEC founded (Baghdad, 1960) by Saudi Arabia, Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Venezuela — response to IOC price cuts; Six-Day War (1967) and Yom Kippur War (1973) trigger Arab oil embargo; price quadruples from $3 to $12/bbl; US gasoline rationing; energy security enters national security doctrine; strategic petroleum reserve (SPR) created (1975).

1979–1986

Iranian Revolution & Price Collapse

Iranian Revolution (1979) removes 5M bbl/day from market; second oil crisis; WTI hits $35/bbl (1980, ~$130 in 2024 dollars); Reagan-era deregulation removes US price controls; oil glut develops; OPEC cohesion breaks; price collapses to $10/bbl (1986); cyclical nature of oil markets established in modern memory.

1990s–2000s

Deepwater & Environmental

Gulf War (1990–91); North Sea development peaks; deepwater technology advances (Perdido at 9,627 ft depth, 2010); BP Deepwater Horizon blowout (2010) — 11 deaths, 4.9M barrels spilled; IPCC climate reports (1990 onward) begin establishing scientific consensus; Kyoto Protocol (1997) first international climate framework.

2008–2016

Shale Revolution

Mitchell Energy cracks the Barnett Shale code (2002, commercialized by Devon Energy acquisition); horizontal drilling + hydraulic fracturing at scale; US production rises from 5M to 13M bbl/day (2008–2019); OPEC launches price war (2014) to kill shale — fails; WTI goes negative (-$37/bbl) in April 2020 as COVID destroys demand; shale's variable cost structure proves resilient.

2021–Present

Energy Transition Tension

IEA "no new oil fields" for 1.5°C (May 2021); demand hits record 103M bbl/day (2023) contradicting transition narrative; Exxon acquires Pioneer ($60B, 2024); Chevron acquires Hess ($53B); Houthi Red Sea attacks reroute tankers; Russia-Ukraine war restructures European energy; methanol ships ordered; IRA (US Inflation Reduction Act, 2022) accelerates clean energy but does not replace oil.

"The Prize: The Epic Quest for Oil, Money & Power"Daniel Yergin, 1991Definitive history of the oil industry from 1859 to 1991; Pulitzer Prize winner; explains geopolitics, technology, and economics of oil; essential intellectual foundation for anyone in energy; updated in "The New Map" (2020).
"Hubbert's Peak / Peak Oil Theory"M. King Hubbert, 1956Predicted US oil production would peak in 1970 (correct); bell-curve depletion model; drove energy policy for 50 years; shale revolution challenged but did not invalidate the underlying geology; foundational for reserve depletion analysis.
