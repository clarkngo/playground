How to use the tables

Plain English is a rough translation, not a formal definition. Shows up in points you to the chapter where the term is used most.

Business & ad tech

Term Plain English Shows up in
AttributionLinking a sale or action back to the ad or touchpoints that influenced it.Ch 2, 4
Auction (RTB)Automated instant price discovery for a single ad opportunity — often under 100 ms.Ch 2
CPC / CPM / CPACost per click / per thousand impressions / per acquisition (pay when that event happens).Ch 2
CTRClicks divided by impressions — how often people click after seeing an ad.Ch 2
DSPSoftware advertisers use to buy ad inventory, usually in automated auctions.Ch 2
GMVTotal sales dollars flowing through the marketplace (not the same as ad revenue alone).Ch 2–5
ImpressionOne display of an ad to a user (one load or slot).Ch 2–4
PLA / PLS / SFANames of promoted-listings and seller-funded programs — different pricing rules.Ch 2
ROASReturn on ad spend — roughly revenue or GMV per dollar of ad cost.Ch 2
RTBReal-time bidding — buying/selling impressions one auction at a time, very fast.Ch 2
SSPSoftware publishers use to offer and manage inventory to buyers.Ch 2

Data platform & storage

Term Plain English Shows up in
ClickHouseColumn-oriented database built for fast analytics on huge event tables.Ch 3
ElasticsearchSearch and log index — full-text queries over large document volumes.Ch 3
FlinkStream processor: continuous computation over Kafka with state and windows.Ch 1, 3
HDFSDistributed file system — cheap, scalable storage for raw and processed files.Ch 1, 3
Hive metastoreCatalog of tables (names, columns, where files live) shared by SQL engines.Ch 3
KafkaDurable event log: producers append; many consumers read at their own pace.Ch 1, 3
ParquetColumnar file format — efficient for analytics scans.Ch 3
Partition / topicKafka: topic = named stream; partition = ordered slice for parallelism.Ch 3
SparkBatch and large-scale analytics engine (ETL, SQL, ML) over clusters.Ch 1, 3
Thrift serverLong-lived service that accepts SQL sessions (JDBC/ODBC-style) and runs them on Spark.Ch 3
YARNCluster resource manager — who gets CPU/memory for jobs.Ch 3, Spark supplement
