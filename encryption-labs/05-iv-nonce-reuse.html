IV / Nonce Reuse

What breaks when the same IV is used twice

Scenario: A bank transmits two wire-transfer orders encrypted with AES-CTR. The developer used the same key — but forgot to generate a fresh nonce for each message. An attacker who intercepts both ciphertexts can recover the secret amounts without knowing the key.

Wire 2??? 819A64EC4C42216963C6E60B419E4CC7

🔍 Attacker's move — XOR the two ciphertexts:

C₁ ⊕ C₂ = 00000000000000000000080909090000
P₁ ⊕ P₂ = 00000000000000000000080909090000 (identical — key cancels out!)
💥 Attacker knows Wire 1 = "TRANSFER $1000.0" → XOR recovers Wire 2: "TRANSFER $9999.0"

💡 Why it matters

Nonce reuse in CTR/GCM mode is the "two-time pad" attack. In AES-GCM specifically, reusing a nonce also exposes the authentication key — completely breaking both confidentiality and integrity. The fix is simple: always use os.urandom(12) to generate a fresh nonce per encryption. Never hardcode or increment a counter without proper overflow handling.
