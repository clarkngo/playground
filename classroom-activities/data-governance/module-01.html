📄 Case Study: “RetailCorp: The Data Chaos Challenge”

A mid-to-large global retailer has grown rapidly across e-commerce, physical stores, mobile, and third-party marketplaces — leaving behind fragmented data, conflicting definitions, and zero governance. Your consulting team has been hired to fix it.

📚 Why this answer? ►

Why is the CDO at the top?

Data governance only works when it has executive authority. Without a CDO, cross-domain conflicts (e.g., Sales and Marketing disagreeing on what a "Customer" is) have no neutral arbiter. The CDO is not a technical role — they are the business accountable for data as a corporate asset, reporting to the C-suite just like a CFO is accountable for financial assets.

Why are Data Owners business leaders, not IT?

IT manages the storage and access of data; it does not own the meaning or quality. If the CRM Product Manager is not accountable for what "Customer" means, no amount of technical tooling will fix the inconsistency. Ownership must sit with the team that understands and depends on the data for business decisions. IT is an enabler, not an owner.

Why one owner per domain, not one owner for all data?

Shared ownership is effectively no ownership. When everyone is responsible, no one is. Splitting ownership by domain (Customer, Sales, Marketing) gives each domain a single accountable person. It also reflects how the business is actually organized — the Sales Ops Manager has no authority over marketing campaign data, and they should not be asked to govern it.

Why separate Stewards from Owners?

Owners are senior stakeholders who set direction and approve decisions — they cannot monitor data quality daily. Stewards are the operational layer: they enforce the rules the owner sets, tag PII fields, update metadata, and flag anomalies. This separation keeps governance scalable as the organization grows without constantly escalating to senior leadership for routine maintenance tasks.

Why list Consumers at the bottom?

Consumers are the end-users of the governance structure. They benefit from the trust and consistency that governance creates, but they are not decision-makers in it. Placing them at the bottom of the hierarchy is not hierarchical in a negative sense — it reflects the direction of accountability: decisions flow top-down, quality issues are reported bottom-up.

📚 Why this answer? ►

Why does a Business Glossary exist at all?

RetailCorp's core problem is that the word "Customer" triggers three different SQL queries depending on which team you ask. A Business Glossary is the organization's official dictionary — it breaks the cycle of local redefinitions by making one definition the authoritative source that all reports, pipelines, and dashboards must reference. Without it, every team continues building on their own assumptions.

Why is "Customer" defined by purchase, not registration?

The definition must be actionable and tied to business value. A registered user who never purchased has zero revenue impact. Defining "Customer" as someone with at least one confirmed purchase aligns the term with how finance, sales, and leadership actually use it when discussing performance. It also resolves the ambiguity: the POS and E-commerce Platform both record purchases, so the authoritative source is clear.

Why does Revenue explicitly exclude tax and shipping?

Online orders collect sales tax; in-store orders sometimes do not. Shipping exists on e-commerce but not POS. If revenue includes these in some reports but not others, two analysts pulling "monthly revenue" will always get different numbers — and both will be technically correct. The standard definition removes the ambiguity by fixing the formula: SUM(order_amount) for completed orders, nothing else. Any deviation must be annotated explicitly.

Why is Product in the Customer Domain, not Sales?

Products exist before any sale occurs. They are catalog entities — created, described, and managed by merchandising and product teams, not the sales operation. Placing Product under the Customer Data Owner (CRM Product Manager) reflects that product catalog management is a product/customer-experience function. Sales data references products; it does not own them.

What happens if a conflict note is missing from a glossary term?

If you publish a new standard definition without documenting the old conflicting ones, teams that have been using their local definition will not know they need to change anything. The conflict note serves as the institutional memory: it explains why the standard definition was chosen, which helps teams understand and adopt it instead of reverting to their old behavior.

📚 Why this answer? ►

Why register data assets in a catalog at all?

Without a catalog, finding data is a word-of-mouth process. A new analyst asks a colleague, who asks another colleague, who may have left the company. A metadata catalog like OpenMetadata makes every table and column discoverable, searchable, and documented — so anyone can answer "where does customer data live and who owns it?" in seconds, not days.

Why document at the column level, not just the table level?

Tables tell you what data exists. Columns tell you what it means. The difference between order_amount being pre-tax vs. post-tax, or signup_date being UTC vs. local time, completely changes how you use the data. Column-level documentation also enables impact analysis: if the email column changes type, you can immediately identify every pipeline and dashboard that depends on it.

Why tag PII at the column level?

GDPR Article 30 requires organizations to maintain a Record of Processing Activities (ROPA) — a map of where personal data lives, why it is processed, and who has access. You cannot comply with a data subject request ("delete all my data") if you do not know which columns across which tables contain that person's information. Column-level PII tags in the catalog are the technical foundation of that compliance inventory.

Why use PK and FK tags?

Primary keys define uniqueness guarantees — if customer_id has duplicates, your joins will produce inflated numbers silently. Foreign keys define referential integrity — an orders row with a customer_id that does not exist in customers is an orphaned record that corrupts any joined analysis. Tagging these in the catalog lets data quality tools automatically monitor for violations without relying on tribal knowledge.

Why does Diana's missing order row matter?

Diana is in customers (customer_id = 4) but has no rows in orders. This is not a data quality error — it is expected. But it exposes an important design choice: if you inner join customers to orders, Diana disappears from your analysis. If your customer count comes from the orders table, you will always undercount. This is precisely the scenario that caused RetailCorp's conflicting "total customer" numbers, and it reinforces why the glossary definition ("at least one purchase") must be explicit.

📚 Why this answer? ►

What is a Data Domain and why does it matter?

A data domain is a grouping of data assets by business capability, not by technical system or database. The Customer Domain exists regardless of whether the data lives in a CRM, a data lake, or a flat file. This matters because systems get replaced — your governance structure should survive a CRM migration. Domains also reflect team accountability: the person who knows customer data best is in the customer-facing team, not the DBA.

Why three domains and not more (or fewer)?

The domains follow RetailCorp's natural business functions: who the customer is, what they bought, and how marketing engaged them. Fewer domains would create one owner responsible for too much, losing accountability. More domains would over-fragment governance and create unnecessary overhead for a foundational implementation. As RetailCorp matures, domains like Product, Logistics, or Finance could be added — but starting with the three most conflict-prone areas is the right scoping decision for Module 01.

Why use email to join Customer to Marketing instead of customer_id?

The Marketing System was built independently and does not store customer_id — it only knows email addresses. This is a real-world data integration challenge: two systems that conceptually track the same person use different identifiers. Using email as the bridge key works, but it introduces risk: if a customer changes their email, the link breaks. This cross-domain relationship documents the current state honestly while flagging it as a candidate for future remediation (e.g., injecting customer_id into the Marketing System).

Why document cross-domain relationships explicitly?

Without a documented cross-domain map, every analyst who needs to combine customer and marketing data will write their own join logic, using different keys, different join types, and different filters. The result is multiple versions of "campaign-attributed revenue" that never match. The cross-domain table gives every team a single agreed-upon join specification, so reports are consistent even when built by different people in different tools.

Task 5 Define Governance Policies Deliverable: Policy Document +
Answer — RetailCorp Data Governance Policies v1.0
Policy 1 — Data Ownership
  • Every data asset must have exactly one designated Data Owner accountable for its quality and governance.
  • Data Owners are senior business stakeholders, not IT; ownership is a business accountability, not a technical role.
  • Ownership is assigned at the domain level; day-to-day operations are delegated to Data Stewards.
  • Any data asset without a documented owner must be escalated to the CDO within 30 days of discovery.
  • Ownership transfers require CDO approval and must be reflected in the metadata catalog within 5 business days.
Policy 2 — Data Access
  • Access to data assets is granted by the Data Owner of the relevant domain, not by IT unilaterally.
  • Default access for all employees is read-only to non-PII aggregated data.
  • Access to PII fields (name, email) requires written approval from the Domain Data Owner with a stated business justification.
  • Access requests must be reviewed within 5 business days; all approvals are logged in the catalog with a timestamp and justification.
  • Access is automatically revoked after 90 days of inactivity and must be re-requested to be reinstated.
Policy 3 — Definition Standardization
  • All business terms used in reports, dashboards, and data pipelines must reference the RetailCorp Business Glossary as the authoritative source.
  • No team may apply a local redefinition of a governed glossary term without submitting a formal change request.
  • New term proposals are submitted to the relevant Domain Owner, reviewed within 10 business days, and published to the glossary upon approval.
  • Conflicting definitions discovered in existing systems must be flagged by the Steward and resolved within the quarter of discovery.
  • All ETL pipelines joining or transforming glossary-governed fields must reference the canonical definition in the pipeline documentation.
📚 Why this answer? ►

Why are governance policies necessary if you already have a glossary and catalog?

A glossary defines what things mean. A catalog shows where they live. But neither document tells anyone what they must do and who is accountable when things go wrong. Policies are the enforcement layer that converts good intentions into organizational behavior. Without them, teams revert to their old habits the moment a governance champion moves on or gets busy with something else.

Why is data ownership a business accountability, not an IT ticket?

IT teams are accountable for uptime, security, and storage — not for whether the order_amount column correctly excludes refunds. If ownership is assigned to IT, the person who decides what "correct" means is the DBA, not the Sales Ops Manager who presents revenue to the board. Governance policy must explicitly state that ownership sits with the business domain expert, or organizations will default to assigning it to whoever manages the database.

Why 90-day automatic access revocation?

The principle of least privilege requires that access to sensitive data (especially PII) is only granted for as long as it is actively needed. In practice, people change roles, finish projects, or leave teams but retain their old data access indefinitely. A 90-day expiry forces a regular review cycle without requiring manual audits. This is a standard control in SOC 2, ISO 27001, and GDPR compliance frameworks — organizations that lack it routinely fail security audits.

Why require a formal change request to modify a glossary term?

The root cause of RetailCorp's original problem was informal, undocumented redefinitions: each team quietly changed what "Customer" meant for their own purposes with no coordination. A formal change request process creates a paper trail, forces cross-domain review, and ensures the teams most affected by a definition change are consulted before it is made. Without this, the glossary itself will become inconsistent over time as teams find workarounds.

Why must ETL pipelines reference the canonical definition in documentation?

A pipeline that joins on customer_id and filters for order_amount > 0 is silently implementing the "Customer = at least one purchase" definition in code. If the glossary definition changes but the pipeline does not, the pipeline becomes incorrect — and no one will know unless the documentation explicitly links the two. Traceability between business definitions and technical implementations is what makes governance auditable and maintainable.

📚 Why this answer? ►

Why use inferSchema=True instead of defining the schema manually?

For profiling and exploration, inferSchema=True is acceptable — Spark reads the data and guesses types automatically, which is fast for small datasets. In production pipelines, you should always define the schema explicitly. If a source system starts sending order_amount as a string instead of a decimal (a common upstream change), inferSchema will silently infer the wrong type and corrupt downstream aggregations without raising an error.

Why use a LEFT join instead of an INNER join?

An INNER join would silently drop Customer 4 (Diana) from the result because she has no matching rows in orders. During data profiling, silent data loss is the most dangerous outcome — you do not know what you are missing. A LEFT join retains all customers with NULL values for order columns, making the gap visible. This is how you discover that 25% of your customers (1 out of 4) have never placed an order — a potentially important business insight that an INNER join would have hidden.

Why use left_anti specifically to find unmatched customers?

A LEFT join with a WHERE orders.order_id IS NULL filter achieves the same result, but left_anti is more explicit in its intent: "give me every row from the left table that has NO match in the right table." It is also more performant on large datasets because Spark can optimize it differently from a filter-after-join. More importantly, naming the operation left_anti communicates the data quality check intent clearly to anyone reading the notebook.

Why aggregate by customer_id before joining to the customers table?

If you join first and then aggregate, you risk a cartesian product when a customer has multiple orders — their name and email would appear duplicated once per order in the joined result. Aggregating on orders_df first produces one row per customer (with totals), and then joining to customers for names is clean and correct. This is a fundamental pattern in data engineering: reduce before joining, not after.

Why run describe() as part of profiling?

describe() returns count, mean, standard deviation, min, and max for numeric columns. For governance, the most useful signal is the count: if describe() shows 3 rows for order_amount but 4 rows for order_id, you have a null in a column that should not be nullable — a data quality violation. This is your first automated signal that a governance rule (e.g., "order_amount must not be null") is being violated in the source data.

Reflection
Why does data governance matter? What would happen to RetailCorp without it?

Without governance, RetailCorp operates with five independent definitions of "Customer" — meaning the sales team, marketing team, and finance team will always produce different numbers when asked the same question. Leadership cannot make confident decisions when every report contradicts the others.

The compliance risk is immediate: PII fields like name and email sit in tables accessible to any analyst. Under GDPR and CCPA, this is not hypothetical — it is a regulatory violation in waiting. Governance creates the access controls, PII tagging, and audit trails required to demonstrate compliance and respond to data subject requests.

Data governance is ultimately a trust infrastructure. By assigning ownership, publishing a business glossary, and registering assets in a central catalog, RetailCorp gives every employee a single source of truth. Analysts spend less time arguing about definitions and more time generating insights. Data becomes a reliable, auditable asset rather than a liability.
