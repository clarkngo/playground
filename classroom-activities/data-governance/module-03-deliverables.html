Why are email and SSN sensitive?
Email addresses and Social Security Numbers (SSNs) are sensitive data because they are primary keys to identity theft, financial fraud, and unauthorized access to personal accounts. SSNs act as a master key for credit and government services, while emails serve as universal digital identifiers for password resets and login credentials, making them high-value targets for hackers and cybercriminals.

# Mask Email from pyspark.sql.functions import regexp_replace df_masked = df.withColumn( "email", regexp_replace("email", "(^.).*(@.*$)", "\\1***\\2") )
Regex captures first char and domain → j***@gmail.com. The (^.) group keeps exactly one character; (@.*$) preserves the full domain.
In [4]
# Mask SSN df_masked = df_masked.withColumn( "ssn", regexp_replace("ssn", "\\d{3}-\\d{2}", "***-**") )
Pattern \d{3}-\d{2} matches the first two SSN segments → ***-**-6789. Last 4 digits retained for record linkage without re-identifying the individual.

Design choice — partial masking over full redaction: Keeping the first character of the email and the last 4 digits of the SSN preserves enough signal for record matching and customer service use cases without exposing re-identifiable data. A fully redacted field (e.g., REDACTED) breaks downstream joins; partial masking is the HIPAA Safe Harbor standard for de-identification per 45 CFR §164.514(b).

⚙ Process — How It Was Built

🔒
Step 1 — Identify the Breach Surface
Audited the raw patients table and identified 15 columns, 2 of which (email, SSN) are high-risk PII re-identifiers and 3 (diagnosis, medication, insurance_id) are Confidential PHI. The remaining columns were assessed as Public or Low sensitivity.
⚡
Step 2 — Databricks Masking Notebook
Built a 7-cell PySpark notebook to load the CSV, apply partial masking to email and SSN using regexp_replace, and persist both the raw and masked tables to Unity Catalog under different access policies.
🔗
Step 3 — OpenMetadata RBAC and Classification
Created DataSensitivity classification with PII, Confidential, and Public tags. Tagged all 15 columns in the patients_secure catalog entry. Defined Admin and Analyst roles with explicit allow/deny lists for table routing.
🛡
Step 4 — Governance Policies and Risk Assessment
Authored 5 security policies in OpenMetadata, mapped 3 critical risks on a heat matrix, cited the relevant HIPAA CFR sections, and assessed compliance gaps in both Databricks and OpenMetadata against production HIPAA requirements.

🧠 Key Learnings

Masking is Not De-identification

Partial masking (keeping the first character of email and last 4 digits of SSN) satisfies operational needs but does not meet the HIPAA Safe Harbor standard for full de-identification. Safe Harbor requires removing all 18 PHI identifiers, including date of birth, city, state, and zip code — columns that were left unmasked in this exercise. The notebook addresses the two highest-risk identifiers only.

RBAC is Layered, Not Singular

Databricks Unity Catalog RBAC (table-level isolation) and OpenMetadata RBAC (catalog metadata permissions) are two separate enforcement planes. A user blocked in OpenMetadata can still query Databricks directly via SQL if their Unity Catalog role allows it. True access control requires enforcement at the compute layer, not just the catalog layer.

Tags Enable Discovery, Not Enforcement

Column-level DataSensitivity tags in OpenMetadata are metadata annotations — they communicate intent to human governance teams and drive dashboards, but they do not block queries. Actual enforcement happens at the Databricks SQL policy layer. The two systems must be coordinated: tagging without enforcement is documentation without security.

The BAA Gap

The most important compliance lesson: all the technical controls implemented here are correct in pattern but illegal in production without a Business Associate Agreement. Healthcare governance is not just a technical problem; it is a contractual and legal one. No amount of masking replaces the BAA.

🛑 Roadblocks & Pain Points

OpenMetadata Policy vs. Databricks Unity Catalog
The two RBAC systems are completely independent. A role defined in OpenMetadata has no effect on what a user can actually query in Databricks. Students who assumed the catalog RBAC would enforce data access discovered this gap when they could still run SELECT * FROM patients regardless of their OpenMetadata role assignment.
Regex Scope — SSN Pattern Only Masks Two Segments
The pattern \\d{3}-\\d{2} targets the first two SSN segments (XXX-XX). It does not match non-hyphenated formats (e.g., 123456789) or international ID numbers. Production masking would require a more robust pattern with validation before deployment.
Academic Cluster — No BAA, No Production PHI
The shared Databricks workspace does not have a signed BAA with the university. This means real patient data cannot lawfully be used even with all technical controls in place. The Patients_500.csv file used here is synthetic; production deployment would require a dedicated, BAA-covered workspace.

Healthcare data governance is the highest-stakes version of data governance. Every gap — an unmasked column, a missing tag, an unsigned BAA — is not just a technical debt item. It is a potential HIPAA violation with civil and criminal consequences. This module demonstrated the minimum viable governance stack: masking at compute, classification at the catalog, and RBAC at both layers. In production, none of these steps are optional.
