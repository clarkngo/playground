Database Sharding Plan

📋 Scenario

A messaging platform currently stores 2 billion user records and is growing at 40 million new users per month. Each user record is 2 KB on average. A single shard can hold 200 GB of data at a comfortable 70% fill level (above that, query performance degrades). The team must decide how many shards to provision now and plan for 3 years of growth — including a 20% buffer for hot spots and index overhead.

📊 Given Values

Current Users
2 billion
Growth Rate
40M / month
Record Size
2 KB
Shard Capacity
200 GB
Max Fill Level
70%
Overhead Buffer
20%
Unit Reference: 1 GB = 1,073,741,824 bytes ≈ 1,000,000 KB (use 1,000,000 for simplicity) | Effective shard capacity = 200 GB × 70% fill × (1 − 20% overhead) | Shards needed = ⌈total data / effective shard capacity⌉ | Future users = current + (growth/month × 36 months)

🧮 Step-by-Step Calculation

Step 1 — Current total data size (GB)
Formula: 2,000,000,000 records × 2 KB / 1,000,000 KB per GB
GB
Step 2 — Effective usable storage per shard (GB)
Formula: 200 GB × 0.70 (fill limit) × 0.80 (overhead buffer)
GB/shard
Step 3 — Shards needed TODAY (round up)
Formula: ⌈current data GB / effective GB per shard⌉
shards
Step 4 — Total users in 3 years
Formula: 2,000,000,000 + (40,000,000 × 36 months)
users
Step 5 — Total data in 3 years (GB)
Formula: future users × 2 KB / 1,000,000
GB
Step 6 — Shards needed in 3 years (round up)
Formula: ⌈future data GB / effective GB per shard⌉
shards
Step 7 — Additional shards to add over the next 3 years
Formula: shards in 3 years − shards today
new shards

✅ Worked Solution

Current data = 2B × 2KB / 1,000,000 = 4,000 GB (4 TB)
Eff. per shard = 200 × 0.70 × 0.80 = 112 GB/shard
Shards today = ⌈4,000 / 112⌉ = ⌈35.7⌉ = 36 shards
Users in 3yr = 2B + (40M × 36) = 2B + 1.44B = 3.44 billion
Data in 3yr = 3.44B × 2KB / 1,000,000 = 6,880 GB
Shards in 3yr = ⌈6,880 / 112⌉ = ⌈61.4⌉ = 62 shards
New shards = 62 − 36 = 26 additional shards
Engineer's Takeaway: Re-sharding is extremely painful — it requires migrating data across shards (often with dual-writes and a long cutover window). The best time to over-provision shards is at the start. Many teams choose a shard count that's a power of 2 (e.g., 64 or 128) to allow future resharding by splitting each shard in half without rehashing the entire keyspace.
