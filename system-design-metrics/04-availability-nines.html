Availability & The Nines

📋 Scenario

Your SRE team has signed an SLA with enterprise customers guaranteeing 99.95% availability. Before finalising the contract, the legal team needs to know exactly how much downtime that represents per year, per month, per week, and per day — and what it would take to achieve an extra "nine" at 99.995%.

Convert the SLA percentage into concrete downtime budgets for each time horizon.

📊 Given Values

SLA Uptime %
99.95%
Minutes / Year
525,600
Minutes / Month
43,800
Unit Reference: 1 year = 525,600 min | 1 month ≈ 43,800 min | 1 week = 10,080 min | 1 day = 1,440 min | Downtime % = (100 − SLA%) | Downtime min = Total min × (Downtime% / 100)

min/day

Step 7 — How many more 9s does 99.999% give vs 99.95%?
Count the decimal nines: 99.95% has how many nines? 99.999% has how many? Difference?
extra 9(s)

✅ Worked Solution

Downtime% = 100 − 99.95 = 0.05%
Per year = 525,600 × 0.0005 = 262.8 min/yr (~4h 22m)
Per month = 43,800 × 0.0005 = 21.9 min/mo
Per week = 10,080 × 0.0005 = 5.04 min/wk
Per day = 1,440 × 0.0005 = 0.72 min/day (~43 sec)
99.999%/yr = 525,600 × 0.00001 = 5.256 min/yr (~5 min)
Extra nines: 99.95% = 3.something nines → 99.999% = 5 nines → difference = 2 extra nines
Engineer's Takeaway: Each additional "nine" reduces your downtime budget by roughly 10×. Going from three 9s (99.9% = 8.7 hr/yr) to four 9s (99.99% = 52 min/yr) is manageable with redundancy. Five 9s (99.999% = 5.26 min/yr) demands active–active multi-region with automated failover — a fundamentally different architecture.
