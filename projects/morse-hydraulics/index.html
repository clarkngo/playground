What Is This Industry
⚙️

Hydraulic Systems

Use pressurized fluid to transmit force. Found in construction equipment, industrial machinery, agriculture, manufacturing, aerospace, and vehicles. Hoses and fittings are consumable components — they fail under pressure, heat, and wear.

🏪

Hose & Fitting Distributors

Source hoses and fittings from manufacturers, cut and crimp hoses to spec, and sell over-the-counter or via mobile services. Speed and breadth of inventory are key competitive advantages.

🚨

Emergency Repair Context

A blown hydraulic hose on a job site can halt $50k/day of equipment. Customers need fast replacement — often same-day or within hours. This makes the "hard to find fittings" value prop critical.

📐

Custom Fabrication

Unlike commodity retail, hose assemblies are often custom — specific length, end fittings, pressure rating. Staff need technical knowledge to match specifications from worn or unlabeled parts.

Domain Knowledge to Walk In With
🔤

STAMPED — The Selection Framework

Every time a customer walks in with a failed hose, staff mentally run through STAMPED: Size, Temperature, Application, Material, Pressure, Ends (couplings), Delivery. This is the industry standard checklist. Know this acronym — it will come up.

📏

Dash Numbers = Hose Size

Hose size is in dash numbers — sixteenths of an inch of inside diameter. -8 hose = 8/16 = 1/2" ID. -12 = 3/4". -16 = 1". Staff will say things like "I need a -8 G2, 36 inches." You now know what that means.

🏗️

Hose Construction: 3 Layers

Tube (inner conduit, sets the ID), Reinforcement (braided wire, spiral wire, or helical — gives strength), Cover (outer protection from abrasion, weather, chemicals). Spiral = higher pressure. Braided = most common.

🔌

Thread Standards — The Compatibility Trap

JIC (37° flare), NPT (tapered pipe thread), BSP (British), SAE O-ring — these are NOT interchangeable. Getting the wrong fitting type causes leaks or failures. This is one of the hardest things for customers to self-identify.

🧾

Reading the Layline

Every hose has a layline — printed text along the side showing brand, hose type (e.g. "G2"), dash size, and pressure rating. When a customer brings in a failed hose, the first thing staff do is read the layline. If it is worn off, they cut and measure the ID.

⚡

Most Common Hose: G2

G2 is the most popular hydraulic hose — used on backhoes, forklifts, construction equipment, trash trucks, farm equipment. If you see hoses on the shelf, most will be G2. High-pressure, two-wire braid, SAE 100R2 spec. Know this one cold.

🔧

Crimping = The Core Skill

After selecting the hose and fitting, staff cut the hose to length and crimp the fittings on using a crimper machine and die sets matched to the hose/fitting combo. Wrong die = under- or over-crimp = failure. Watch for how they select dies — this is where errors happen.

💥

Pressure: Working vs. Burst

Working pressure = max safe operating pressure. Burst pressure = 4x working pressure (safety factor). A hose rated 3,000 PSI working has ~12,000 PSI burst. Staff must match or exceed system pressure — never underspec. Pressure spikes (impulse) also matter — they can exceed working pressure momentarily.

Mindset for Tomorrow
✅

Do: Be a student first

Ask staff to walk you through what they do. Say "I am learning the business — can you show me how you identify a hose?" People love to explain their craft. You learn more and build rapport.

✅

Do: Count the friction

Every time someone pauses, walks away, makes a phone call, flips through a binder, or says "let me check" — that is a data point. These are your AI opportunity markers. Note them.

✅

Do: Ask about the worst days

"What is the most stressful situation you deal with at the counter?" surfaces pain faster than any structured question. Emergency calls + wrong part + unhappy customer = the real pressure points.

⚠️

Do not: Pitch AI yet

This visit is discovery only. Do not mention specific AI solutions. If they ask, say "I want to understand your operation fully before I suggest anything." Pitching before listening destroys trust.

⚠️

Do not: Fake expertise

If they use a term you do not know, ask them to explain it. "I am not from the industry — what does that mean?" is a strength, not a weakness. It shows intellectual honesty and invites teaching.

⚠️

Do not: Jump to solutions

Even if you see an obvious pain point, do not immediately say "we could fix that with AI." Write it down mentally. Validate it with a second or third observation before treating it as a real opportunity.
