💡 Why it matters

In 2009, researchers demonstrated a timing attack against HMAC in Python that recovered keys over a network in ~300 requests per byte. Python's == operator short-circuits on the first differing character — making comparisons faster for "more wrong" guesses and slower for "more correct" ones. By sending thousands of variations and measuring response times in microseconds, an attacker recovers the expected HMAC one byte at a time. Fix: replace sig == expected with hmac.compare_digest(sig, expected) — it compares all bytes in constant time regardless of where they differ.
