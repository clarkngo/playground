💡 Why it matters

RC4 was used in WEP (Wi-Fi Encryption Protocol) — cracked in under 60 seconds using freely available tools. It was later used in WPA-TKIP (broken in 2009) and in TLS cipher suites (deprecated by RFC 7465 in 2015). RC4's keystream has statistical biases in the first 256 bytes: the second byte of output is twice as likely to be 0 as any other value. These biases enable plaintext recovery without brute-force. DES requires significant hardware to break; RC4 is broken by pure cryptanalysis. Fix: replace DES → AES-256-GCM, RC4 → ChaCha20-Poly1305 or AES-256-GCM, MD4 → SHA-256 or BLAKE2b.
