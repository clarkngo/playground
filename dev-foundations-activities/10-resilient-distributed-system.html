Module 10 — Resiliency

Resilient Distributed System Design

Why This Matters
The Problem

Distributed systems fail in partial, unpredictable ways. A single slow downstream service can exhaust all threads. A node crash without replication means data loss. Without resilience patterns, cascading failures take down the whole system.

The Solution

Defense-in-depth resiliency: traffic is load-balanced, circuit breakers stop cascades, retries with backoff recover from transient failures, bulkheads isolate blast radius, and replicas provide CAP-aware redundancy.

Real-World Use

Netflix Hystrix popularized circuit breakers. AWS Multi-AZ uses replica sets. Chaos Engineering (Netflix Chaos Monkey) validates resilience under real failure conditions. These patterns are foundational to SRE practice.

Your Goal

Understand why resilience is layered — each pattern addresses a different failure mode — and why chaos testing must validate the whole system together.

🛡 Resilient Failure vs Cascading Failure Simulator
The Objective: You are designing the resiliency architecture for a distributed production system. Sequence the patterns and components from the outermost traffic layer through fault isolation, recovery mechanisms, data redundancy, and finally system-wide chaos validation.

Think about: What is the first component traffic hits? How does a circuit breaker differ from a retry policy? What is a bulkhead pattern and why does it prevent cascading failures? Where does chaos testing fit in the stack? Watch out for decoys that represent single points of failure.

📄 Component Reference

▼
⚖Load Balancer
Distributes traffic across multiple healthy instances and removes unhealthy ones from rotation. The first resilience layer that traffic encounters.
💡 Traffic distribution is always the outermost resilience concern.
⚡Circuit Breaker
Monitors call failure rates to a downstream service. When failures exceed a threshold, it opens the circuit and fast-fails requests, preventing thread exhaustion and cascading failures.
💡 The circuit breaker sits between the load balancer and retry logic.
🔄Retry with Backoff
Automatically retries failed requests with exponential backoff and jitter. Recovers from transient failures without overwhelming the downstream service with immediate retries.
💡 Retries only make sense when the circuit is closed and the failure may be transient.
🔞Bulkhead Isolation
Partitions resources (thread pools, connection pools) so that a failing service can only exhaust its own pool, not the shared pool. Limits blast radius of partial failures.
💡 Bulkheads isolate failures after retry policies have been applied.
📂Replica Set (CAP-aware)
Multiple copies of data distributed across availability zones or regions. Provides redundancy for data layer failures. CAP theorem governs consistency vs availability tradeoffs under partition.
💡 Data replication sits below application-layer resilience patterns.
📊Distributed Tracing
End-to-end request tracing across services (Jaeger, Zipkin, AWS X-Ray). Identifies failure hotspots and latency in production so teams can validate resilience in real conditions.
💡 Tracing instruments the full system to make failures visible before chaos testing.
💀Chaos Testing Framework
Deliberately injects failures (process kills, network delays, disk full) to validate that all the resilience layers above actually work under real conditions (e.g., Chaos Monkey, Gremlin).
💡 Chaos testing validates the whole stack and must come last in the design.
