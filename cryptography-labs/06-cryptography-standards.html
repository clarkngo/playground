NIST security levels, algorithm lifecycle, FIPS 140-3 compliance, and cryptographic agility

You need to assess which algorithms are approved, plan for post-quantum migration, and ensure the design supports cryptographic agility — the ability to swap algorithms without a full system rewrite.

💡 Grover's Algorithm: Halves the effective key length for symmetric ciphers. AES-128 drops to 64-bit quantum security (Level 1). AES-256 stays at 128-bit quantum security (Level 5) — still infeasible. This is why AES-256 is recommended for long-term data protection.

💡 Cryptographic Agility: Design systems so algorithms are configurable, not hardcoded. Abstract your crypto into a provider layer — when SHA-256 eventually weakens, you swap the provider, not your entire codebase. PKCS#11, JCA/JCE, and OpenSSL EVP all support this pattern.

A US federal agency must choose symmetric encryption for classified data. Which combination meets FIPS 140-3 requirements?

📚 FIPS 140-3 Algorithm Requirements
  • Symmetric encryption: AES (128, 192, or 256-bit) required. DES, 3DES, and RC4 are not approved for new systems.
  • Approved modes: CBC, CTR, GCM (ECB is approved but forbidden for most uses). GCM provides built-in authentication.
  • Hash functions: SHA-2 (SHA-256, SHA-384, SHA-512) or SHA-3. MD5 and SHA-1 are not approved for any security use.
  • Authentication: HMAC-SHA-256 or AES-GCM authenticated encryption. Unauthenticated encryption is prohibited for classified data.

Option B (AES-128-GCM + SHA-256) is the only option that uses an approved algorithm, an approved authenticated mode, and an approved hash function.
